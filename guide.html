Instructor guide

Running the Test Lab.

A step-by-step web tool that walks each group through the Camera Testing Handbook. It builds their exposure ladder for their camera, tells them how to meter every rung, and turns results into rules and a printable packet.

The link to share https://cfacontact.github.io/film393-overunder-lab/

Before Tuesday · 10 minutes

  1. Open the lab and click “See a finished example.” It loads your original F3 test: the 45° / one-double ladder, your readings and your conclusions. Click through all 13 steps so you know what students will see. Click “Start my own group” when you’re done.
  2. Check the Speedmaster wording on step 5. The lab tells students to use the meter’s cine mode where you enter frame rate and shutter angle (HD Cine). If the SCAD meters label it differently, tell students on the day.
  3. Handbook conflicts. The lab follows your rules: no ND, no lens changes (the lens stays at N for every take, half-stops come from single scrims or the camera’s own shutter angles), and tungsten and scrims only. The printed handbook still says “change T-stop first” on page 4, “an LED you can dim up” on page 3, and splits the lens for half-stops in its example. Mention this in class or update the PDF.
  4. Scrim bags. Every key light needs its doubles and at least one single. The lab uses the single for the half-stop takes, so the lens never moves.
  5. Post it to Blackboard (next section) and create a Blackboard assignment for the PDF packet.

Posting it in Blackboard Ultra

  1. In the course, go to the week’s folder and click + → Create → Link.
  2. Name it Over/Under Test Lab and paste the link above.
  3. Tick open in a new window if it’s offered, then Save.
  4. Create an Assignment named “Camera Test Packet (D1–D4)” for the PDF upload. One per group is fine: add group members in the assignment’s group settings, or have one person submit and list everyone. The packet prints their names.
Why a link, not a file uploadBlackboard often downloads HTML files instead of opening them. The link always opens as a web page on any laptop or phone, with no login.

Class-day flow

WhenLab stepWhat the group does
Before class00–04Group & roles, the variables quiz, camera/lens limits, choose N, check the ladder (all rows ✓).
On stage, first 20 min05Set the Speedmaster to the base fps / shutter / EI shown, put in the base scrims, move the tungsten head until the incident reads N, lock and tape, confirm the grey card on the waveform.
Before rolling06Spot-meter skin, fabric, background, highlights, shadows. Optional: snap a frame and pin the readings on it.
Shooting07Checklist, then 15 takes per profile. Log reads the row aloud → 1st AC sets it → Gaffer confirms scrims → slate → roll for the time shown.
Review / grading08–10One-light (one correction for all takes), best-light (each take rescued), compare profiles, EI advice.
Wrap11–12Write on-set rules (the lab flags rules with no number or with adjectives), then print the packet to PDF.

Student quick start

  1. Open the link on the laptop you’ll use on the stage. Work saves automatically in that browser.
  2. Go through the steps in order with the Next → button, or jump around with the step list on the left (on a phone, the step chips at the top).
  3. Every step starts with “What are we doing now?”, which says what you’re doing, why it matters, and how you know you’re done. Don’t know a word? Open Words on this page at the bottom, or click Word list at the top.
  4. On the Camera step, every question has a “How do I find this?” link with click-by-click instructions. Use it with the camera in your hands before asking for help.
  5. Blue boxes explain why. Grey “From the original F3 test” boxes show a finished example. Don’t copy those numbers; your camera is different.
  6. On Build the ladder, every row must show a green ✓. If your camera can’t do a setting, type what it can do; the ✓ column re-checks the math.
  7. Type meter readings the way the Speedmaster shows them: 2.8, 4 1/3, 5.6 2/3, or tenths like 2.8 7.
  8. Switching laptops? Packet & export → Export group file, then send the file to your teammate, who clicks Import group file.
  9. When you’re done, Packet & export → Print / Save as PDF and upload it to Blackboard.

Troubleshooting

“My work disappeared.”
It’s saved in the browser it was typed in. Different laptop, a different browser, a private/incognito window, or cleared history will all look empty. Go back to the original laptop, or import the exported group file.
“The ladder step says pick N first.”
Choose a shooting stop on step 03. The ladder is built from it.
“A row is red.”
The settings in that row don’t add up to its rung, or they’re outside the limits entered on step 02. The red label says which. “Reset every row” restores the lab’s suggestion.
“Our camera shows shutter as 1/48, not degrees.”
First try switching the camera to degrees (the “How do I switch to degrees?” link on step 02). If it can’t, set “How does your camera show the shutter?” to fraction of a second. The ladder switches to 1/x values and suggests the nearest speed your menu actually has (e.g. 1/200 for 1/192).
“A take has a ⚠︎ ‘lens moved’ warning.”
Nothing else could reach that take, so the lab had to change the T-stop. The fix is usually one of these: add a single scrim to the count, list every shutter angle including 360°, or widen the frame-rate range. All three are on the Camera step.
“What does ‘Use the lab’s recommended starting settings’ mean?”
The lab picks the shutter angle and scrims already in the light for the N take, choosing the combination that keeps the lens at N and needs the least extra light. Untick it to choose your own. The whole ladder re-plans from your choice.
“The lab wants more light than our head has.”
Lower “Extra light available” on step 02 (its “How do I measure this?” link shows how to test it with the meter). The lab picks starting settings that need less light, and uses frame rate for the far takes.
“The meter column says T0.7.”
That’s the math continuing below the widest stop. On the meter it reads as “under”. It means that rung is very dark, which is expected at N−4 and N−5.
“Photo won’t save.”
Very large photos can fill the browser’s storage. Use a frame grab or a smaller phone photo, and export the group file as a backup.
“The page looks frozen / a click doesn’t work.”
Reload the page (work is saved). If it keeps happening, note which step and tell Prof. Underhill.

Privacy

Nothing students type, and no photos they add, ever leave their laptop. There’s no server, account or tracking. The page itself is public, but it holds no student data. You see their work only through the PDF they upload.

Changing the lab

The lab is one file in the GitHub repo cfacontact/film393-overunder-lab, and it updates within a minute of a change. Ask Claude to change wording, defaults, steps or the example, and the same link keeps working. Students’ saved work is kept across updates.
