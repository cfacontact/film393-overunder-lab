Instructor guide

Running the Test Planner.

A step-by-step web tool that walks each group through the Camera Testing Handbook. It builds their exposure ladder for their camera, tells them how to meter every rung, and turns results into rules and a printable packet.

The main link (each group gets its own version below) https://cfacontact.github.io/film393-overunder-lab/

Before Tuesday · 10 minutes

  1. Open the page and click through all 13 steps so you know what students will see. Try entering one of your cameras.
  2. Metering: students set N by spot-metering the grey card (your preferred method) and double-check with the dome. Step 5 explains the difference between the two.
  3. Check the Speedmaster wording on step 5. Step 5 tells students to use the meter’s cine mode where you enter frame rate and shutter angle (HD Cine). If the SCAD meters label it differently, tell students on the day.
  4. Handbook conflicts. It follows your rules: no ND, no lens changes (the lens stays at N for every take, half-stops come from single scrims or the camera’s own shutter angles), and tungsten and scrims only. The printed handbook still says “change T-stop first” on page 4, “an LED you can dim up” on page 3. Mention this in class or update the PDF.
  5. Calculated as they go. Step 4 calculates the starting shutter angle and scrims and shows the math line by line. Step 5 fills in all 15 takes from those settings. Both update live as students type, and students can change any value (the math check re-checks it). To check a group’s work quickly, look at their step 5: all 14 takes should show green ✓.
  6. Scrim bags. Every key light needs its doubles and at least one single. The half-stop takes use the single, so the lens never moves.
  7. Post one link per group to Blackboard (next section) and create a Blackboard assignment for the PDF packet.

Add the links in Blackboard Ultra

  1. Open your course and go to the week’s folder (or create a folder called Over/Under Test).
  2. Click the + where you want it, then Create → Link.
  3. Name it Over/Under Test Planner · Group 1, paste the Group 1 link, and turn on open in a new window if you see that option. Save.
  4. Repeat for each group. If you use Blackboard groups, you can open each link’s settings and set Release conditions → Membership to that group only, so each group sees just its own link. (Optional. Posting all the links for everyone to see works fine too.)
  5. Create an Assignment named “Camera Test Packet (D1–D4)” for the PDF upload. It can be a group assignment, or one person submits and the packet lists everyone’s names.
Why a link, not a file uploadBlackboard often downloads HTML files instead of opening them. The link always opens as a web page on any laptop or phone, with no login.

Class-day flow

WhenStepWhat the group does
Before class00–03Group and jobs, the variables quiz, camera/lens/scrim limits (with the camera in hand), choose N.
Before class04, Part 1Starting shutter angle and scrims are calculated, with the math shown line by line (stops to open, stops still needed, scrims to pull, the dark side, extra light). The group checks it and can change any setting; everything recalculates as they type.
Before class05All 14 takes are filled in from the starting settings. The group walks through each one and changes anything their camera can’t do; each row re-checks (✓ or “this lands on N+1, not N+2”).
On stage, first 20 min04, Part 2Set the Speedmaster to their N settings, put in the scrims they calculated, spot-meter the grey card to N (dome as a double-check), lock and tape, confirm on the waveform.
Before rolling06Spot-meter skin, background, highlights, shadows (fabric optional). Optional: snap a frame and pin the readings on it.
Shooting07Checklist, then 15 takes. The logger reads the row aloud → First Assistant Camera sets it → gaffer confirms scrims → slate → roll for the time shown.
Review08–10One light, best light, then their profile notes and Exposure Index decision.
Wrap11–12Write on-set rules (rules with no number or with describing words get flagged), then print the packet to PDF.

Student quick start

  1. Open your group’s link on as many laptops or phones as you like. Everyone on the same link sees the same work, live: what one person types appears on everyone else’s screen within a second. The green “● Live” tag at the top shows how many devices are on your link.
  2. Go through the steps in order with the Next → button, or jump around with the step list on the left (on a phone, the step chips at the top).
  3. Every step starts with “What are we doing now?”, which says what you’re doing, why it matters, and how you know you’re done. Don’t know a word? Open Words on this page at the bottom, or click Word list at the top.
  4. Yellow boxes are waiting for your answer. On the Camera step, every question has a “How do I find this?” link. Use it with the camera in your hands before asking for help.
  5. The math is calculated as you type. Blue boxes on steps 4 and 5 are calculated for you; type in one to change it and everything re-checks. Purple “Talk it over” boxes tell you when to stop and explain the math to each other.
  6. Type meter readings the way the Speedmaster shows them: 2.8, 4 1/3, 5.6 2/3, or tenths like 2.8 7.
  7. Switching laptops? Just open your group’s link on the new one: your work is already there.
  8. When you’re done, go to the last step, click Print / Save as PDF, and upload the PDF to Blackboard.

Troubleshooting

“My work disappeared.”
Check that you opened your own group’s link (the number at the end). Work is shared live across every device on that link, and each device also keeps its own copy, so if the connection drops you keep working and it syncs when you’re back online (the tag at the top turns yellow while offline).
“A row says ‘this lands on N+1, not N+2’.”
A value someone typed in that row adds up to a different take. Clear the box to go back to the calculated value, or open “How to think about it” and recount the stops.
“A row says the angle isn’t on our list.”
Only use shutter angles from the list entered on step 2. If the camera really has that angle, add it to the list.
“A take has a ⚠︎ ‘lens moved’ warning.”
That row changes the T-stop, which the class rules avoid. Rethink it with shutter angle and scrims. Step 4 also warns if the starting settings make some takes impossible without moving the lens.
“Our camera shows shutter as 1/48, not degrees.”
First try switching the camera to degrees (the “How do I switch to degrees?” link on step 2). If it can’t, set “How does your camera show the shutter?” to fraction of a second.
“Step 4 says we need more light than we have.”
Choose a larger starting shutter angle (it needs less light), or bring a bigger tungsten light. The “How do I measure this?” link on step 2 shows how to test your extra light with the meter.
“The meter column says T0.7.”
That’s the math continuing below the widest stop. On the meter it reads as “under”. It means that take is very dark, which is expected at N−4 and N−5.
“Photo won’t save.”
Very large photos can fill the browser’s storage. Use a frame grab or a smaller phone photo, and export the group file as a backup.
“The page looks frozen / a click doesn’t work.”
Reload the page (work is saved). If it keeps happening, note which step and tell Prof. Underhill.

How the live sharing works

Each group’s work is stored on a small server in your Railway account (project FILM393 Sync, about $1–3 a month). When a device joins a link, anything it already had is added to the group’s shared copy: empty boxes get filled in, and member names, rules and readings are combined. Nothing already there is ever replaced. Before a device synced for the first time, its own copy was also saved separately on that device as a backup.

Privacy

Student work (including any frame photos) is stored only on your own Railway server, under each group’s link. There are no accounts and no tracking. The page itself is public; anyone who has a group’s link can see and edit that group’s work, so share each link only with its group.

Making changes

The page is one file in the GitHub repo cfacontact/film393-overunder-lab, and it updates within a minute of a change. Ask Claude to change wording, defaults or steps, and the same link keeps working. Students’ saved work is kept across updates.
